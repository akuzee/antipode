Antipode

If the Earth were transparent, what would you see beneath your feet? These apps draw the far side of the planet — live, correctly oriented, locked to the world. Straight down is your exact antipode; the horizon is your own position, in every direction at once.

What are you using?

Everything

  • Antipode AR phone
    Point your phone at the ground: far-side coastlines overlaid on the live camera feed. The flagship app.
  • Earth Window — IMU phone · tablet
    Hold the device like a pane of glass and tilt it to aim through the planet — motion sensors only, no camera, in the spirit of iOS's Measure app.
  • Earth Window — head-tracked laptop
    The screen becomes a literal window: the webcam tracks your head, and the view through the Earth shifts with parallax as you move.
  • Fly Through monitor
    A free-flying 3D Earth. WASD + mouse: dive into the planet, fly the sight-line to the antipode, and look back.
  • The Circle at Your Feet anything
    If the ground were a glass circle of radius r, how much of the far side could you see? Interactive cross-section + far-side map. (Radius = your height ⇒ half the planet.)
  • Arm Aim other
    Batch calculator + map: where to point your arm to aim at any city through the Earth.

Earlier experiments

  • Through the Earth — NYC — the first sketch.

  • Continental Globe — orbitable 3D Earth built from Natural Earth polygons.

  • Day / Night — the globe with a live terminator.
Hand-rolled projection math, no frameworks in the flagship app; coastlines from Natural Earth (public domain). The view through the planet is deliberately mirrored relative to a wall map — you're reading the far surface from the inside.
